# Candidate catalog

All 51 games considered for remakes, generated from `catalog/` by `catalog/tools/make_briefs.py`. The roadmap games come first ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md)); the rest are parked ideas, some of which the stack makes almost free later. Descriptions were drafted from memory and not verified. Names refer to the inspiration only; every remake gets an original title.

## Roadmap

### 1. Boulder Dash

*1984 · First Star Software · Atari 8-bit / C64 · Puzzle / Action · remake effort: small · free-version gap: partly covered* · [brief](games/01-boulderdash.md)

**Loop.** Dig through dirt collecting diamonds before time runs out, while boulders and gems fall under cellular-automaton physics.

**Why remake it.** Grid physics that plays perfectly in 3D: chunky dirt with volumetric crumble, rolling boulders with real weight, dynamic lighting from glowing diamonds, amoeba as animated goo. Rules are tiny and fully deterministic.

**Remake ideas.** The caves could become tactile, lit dioramas with weighty boulder motion, dust and debris particles, glowing gems and dramatic chain-reaction explosions, while the discrete grid simulation stays untouched underneath. Explosion-driven dynamic lighting, camera shake and an online cave editor with leaderboards would fit naturally.

**Modern takes.** Boulder Dash 30th Anniversary (2014) — pleasant but flat; room for a truly premium take

### 2. Rampart

*1990 · Atari Games · Arcade · Puzzle / Strategy · remake effort: small · free-version gap: wide open* · [brief](games/02-rampart.md)

**Loop.** Build walls from Tetris-like pieces to enclose castles, place cannons inside your territory, bombard ships or rivals, repeat.

**Why remake it.** A three-phase loop in one sentence. Modern destruction physics, splashing water and 4-player online play would give it a lot of room to grow. ChatGPT's favourite.

**Remake ideas.** Detailed medieval coastlines with dynamic water, cannon smoke, fire and physically crumbling stone walls would make battles spectacular. Online multiplayer and a readable, tactile building phase would keep the core rules intact.

**Modern takes.** Rampart (GBA/Xbox Live Arcade ports), no real remake

### 3. Fruity Frank

*1984 · Kuma Computers · Amstrad CPC · Maze / Dig · remake effort: small · free-version gap: wide open* · [brief](games/03-fruityfrank.md)

**Loop.** Frank tunnels through his garden collecting fruit, drops apples on the monsters and throws a bouncing ball to stop them.

**Why remake it.** A lush vegetable-garden diorama: soft soil you carve through, glossy fruit, cartoon monsters. Shares its dig-and-crush tech with Boulder Dash and Dig Dug, so it's cheap once one of those exists.

**Remake ideas.** Deformable soil with visible cross-sections, squashy physics on falling apples, and a bouncing ball with a glowing trail. Warm garden lighting and a day-to-dusk cycle across levels.

**Modern takes.** None

### 4. Alley Cat

*1984 · Synapse Software (Bill Williams) · IBM PC · Action / Platform · remake effort: small · free-version gap: wide open* · [brief](games/04-alleycat.md)

**Loop.** A stray cat leaps between fences, trash cans and clotheslines, jumps through open windows into mini-rooms (fishbowl, mice in cheese, sleeping dogs) to win the heart of a lady cat.

**Why remake it.** Great charm potential: a moonlit city alley with warm window light, furry cat animation, and each window room a tiny handcrafted diorama. Short, well-bounded mini-games.

**Remake ideas.** The alley and apartments could become a lively, moonlit cartoon neighborhood with expressive animal animation, physics-driven thrown objects and interactive clutter in each room. Each mini-game room could get its own visual style and lighting while keeping the original rules and timing.

**Modern takes.** None notable

### 5. Winter Games

*1985 · Epyx · C64 / Apple II · Sports / Party · remake effort: medium · free-version gap: wide open* · [brief](games/05-wintergames.md)

**Loop.** A set of winter sports events (ski jump, biathlon, speed skating, figure skating, hot dog aerials, bobsled), each with its own joystick timing or rhythm control, played in turn by up to 8 players.

**Why remake it.** The controls were the fun, so a remake should lean on today's inputs: triggers, gyro aiming, rumble and online play in turn. Snow, ice and alpine lighting are easy wins for modern rendering.

**Remake ideas.** Motion controls, trigger pressure and rumble for each event. Snow and ice shaders, alpine lighting, crowd atmosphere, and online or couch tournaments with national anthems.

**Modern takes.** None faithful; Olympic licensed games are a different style

### 6. Cannon Fodder

*1993 · Sensible Software · Amiga / DOS · Action / Tactics · remake effort: medium · free-version gap: wide open* · [brief](games/06-cannonfodder.md)

**Loop.** Guide a tiny squad with mouse clicks: left to move, right to shoot; grenades and rockets for buildings; recruits die permanently.

**Why remake it.** Top-down diorama style (tilt-shift miniature look) fits perfectly: little soldiers on lush jungle, snow and desert terrain, destructible huts, water shaders, and the famous graveyard hill between missions.

**Remake ideas.** Destructible jungles and buildings, volumetric explosions, dynamic water and weather, plus a detailed, lit Boot Hill memorial, would all hit harder at high fidelity. Online co-op with each player running their own sub-squad would fit the existing split-team system naturally.

**Modern takes.** Cannon Fodder 3 (2011, poorly received) — the space is open

### 7. Double Dragon

*1987 · Technōs Japan · Arcade · Beat 'em up · remake effort: medium · free-version gap: partly covered* · [brief](games/07-doubledragon.md)

**Loop.** Billy and Jimmy Lee fight through a city gang to rescue Marian, with punches, kicks, throws and weapons picked up from enemies.

**Why remake it.** Neon-soaked 80s streets, rain, weapon physics and meaty hit-stop. Two-player co-op is the draw. The combat has to feel heavy, which is animation work more than rules work.

**Remake ideas.** Heavy hit-stop, ragdoll knockdowns, rain-slick neon streets and dynamic crowds. Weapons with real physics and online co-op.

**Modern takes.** Double Dragon Neon (2012), Double Dragon Gaiden (2023)

### 8. Z

*1996 · The Bitmap Brothers · DOS / Mac · RTS · remake effort: large · free-version gap: partly covered* · [brief](games/08-z.md)

**Loop.** No base building: capture territory flags and every factory, radar and gun in the sector becomes yours. More territory means faster production. Robot armies fight for the map.

**Why remake it.** A fast, aggressive RTS with a ton of personality (the sarcastic robots Brad and Allan, cutscenes). Modern tilt-shift battlefields, destructible terrain, great unit animation. Its compact ruleset suits a solo or AI-driven project better than a full RTS.

**Remake ideas.** Detailed, destructible terrain with dynamic lighting and particle effects for explosions and weapons, plus expressive robot animations, would suit a high-end remake. Online multiplayer and ranked matches would fit the fast, aggressive design.

**Modern takes.** Z: Steel Soldiers (2001), mobile ports; no premium remake

## Other candidates

### Pac-Man

*1980 · Namco · Arcade · Maze · remake effort: weekend · free-version gap: partly covered*

**Loop.** Eat every dot in the maze while four ghosts with distinct AI chase you; power pellets turn the tables.

**Why remake it.** The benchmark. Championship Edition (2007) and CE2 proved the formula: neon, speed ramps, ghost trains, pulsing music. Worth studying before building anything else.

**Remake ideas.** Neon-lit mazes with bloom, glowing trails and screen-reactive music (as Championship Edition proved) can amplify the speed and ghost-chain combos, while expressive animated ghosts make their personalities readable. Dynamic camera zoom and particle bursts on ghost eats add spectacle without touching the maze rules.

**Modern takes.** Pac-Man Championship Edition DX / CE2, Pac-Man 99

### Qix

*1981 · Taito · Arcade · Territory / Puzzle · remake effort: weekend · free-version gap: wide open*

**Loop.** Draw lines to claim 75% of the board while the Qix — an abstract swirling line creature — and Sparx try to kill you.

**Why remake it.** Extremely simple and hypnotic. The Qix becomes a gorgeous GPU ribbon/plasma entity; claimed areas reveal a picture or a 3D scene. Perfect for a Tetris Effect–style audiovisual treatment.

**Remake ideas.** The Qix is ideal for a shader showpiece: a fluid, glowing, procedurally animated plasma or ribbon creature, with claimed areas filling in with rich materials, images or reactive light. Satisfying fill animations, particle-edge Sparx and a synced soundtrack could make each claim feel explosive.

**Modern takes.** Volfied, Gals Panic lineage — no premium modern version

### Lemmings

*1991 · DMA Design · Amiga · Puzzle · remake effort: medium · free-version gap: partly covered*

**Loop.** Assign skills (dig, build, block, float…) to mindless lemmings so enough of them reach the exit.

**Why remake it.** Destructible terrain + hundreds of tiny animated characters = stunning in a modern stylized 3D world. Level format is simple; the originals' level design is legendary.

**Remake ideas.** Voxel or physically destructible terrain with high-detail materials would make every bash, dig and explosion look good, and hundreds of expressive lemmings with individual animation and lighting would give the crowd real character. Adding co-op or versus play on shared levels would expand the two-player mode without changing the rules.

**Modern takes.** Lemmings (PSP/PS4 remakes, mobile F2P) — the PSP one was good, now long gone

### Bomberman / Dyna Blaster

*1983 · Hudson Soft · MSX / NES · Maze / Party · remake effort: small · free-version gap: partly covered*

**Loop.** Drop bombs in a grid arena to blast walls and enemies (or friends), collecting range and bomb-count power-ups.

**Why remake it.** Explosions are where modern VFX shines. Local multiplayer makes it the best 'show it to friends' remake. Grid logic is trivial.

**Remake ideas.** Volumetric, physically lit explosions, crumbling destructible blocks and animated arenas would make every blast spectacular, while online and couch multiplayer with rollback netcode would modernize the battle mode. Themed arenas with dynamic lighting could vary the look without changing the grid rules.

**Modern takes.** Super Bomberman R (2017) — the series is alive, so differentiate with style

### Arkanoid

**Modern takes.** Arkanoid – Eternal Battle (2022)

### Scorched Earth

*1991 · Wendell Hicks · DOS · Artillery · remake effort: weekend · free-version gap: partly covered*

**Loop.** Tanks take turns firing with angle and power across destructible terrain, buying absurd weapons between rounds.

**Why remake it.** 'The Mother of All Games'. Destructible voxel terrain plus nukes means huge explosions. Tiny ruleset and a party-game feel.

**Remake ideas.** Fully destructible, physically simulated terrain with high-end explosions, particle effects and dynamic lighting would suit a modern remake. Online multiplayer with many players and custom weapon modding would expand it.

### Magic Carpet / Magic Carpet 2

*1994 · Bullfrog · DOS · Action / Strategy (3D) · remake effort: very large*

**Loop.** Fly a magic carpet over deformable 3D landscapes, cast spells to kill monsters and rival wizards, and gather the scattered mana with your castle's balloons to restore the world's balance.

**Why remake it.** One of the first fully deformable 3D worlds: craters, volcanoes and rising land. Today's GPUs can make its terrain spells, water and fire spectacular, and its mix of shooter and light strategy is still rare.

**Remake ideas.** Heightmap terrain with GPU deformation, volumetric fire and water, big spell effects, flight controls on gamepad, co-op or versus wizards online.

**Modern takes.** None; the originals are sold on GOG

### Big Red Racing

*1996 · Big Red Software / Domark · DOS · Racing (arcade, comic) · remake effort: large*

**Loop.** Arcade races across wildly mixed vehicles (buggies, jeeps, hovercraft, jets, even a moon buggy) on bumpy 3D tracks, with a cartoon, self-mocking tone and split-screen for two.

**Why remake it.** A daft, joyful racer that swaps vehicles and terrain every race. A remake could keep the humour and split-screen and add proper physics, destructible props and online races.

**Remake ideas.** Proper vehicle physics per class, destructible scenery, split-screen and online races, a track editor and a cheeky presentation.
