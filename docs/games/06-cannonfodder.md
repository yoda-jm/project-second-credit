# 6. Cannon Fodder (1993)

*Original: Sensible Software · Amiga / DOS · Action / Tactics. Remake effort: medium. Free-version gap: **wide open**.*

The final title must be an original name (see [../legal.md](../legal.md)).

## Core loop

Guide a tiny squad with mouse clicks: left to move, right to shoot; grenades and rockets for buildings; recruits die permanently.

## Gameplay

You command a small squad of soldiers with the mouse: left-click moves the squad, right-click fires, and holding both buttons throws grenades or fires rockets, both of which come in limited supply. Troops can split into separate groups, board vehicles such as jeeps, tanks, snowmobiles and helicopters, and must clear enemy infantry, destroy buildings that spawn troops, and complete objectives like rescuing hostages or protecting civilians. Soldiers die in a single hit, while survivors are promoted in rank as missions pass.

## Levels

The campaign is 24 missions split into 72 phases, played across jungle, arctic, desert, moorland and underground base settings. Each phase has its own objective, and later ones add vehicles, turrets, quicksand, water and mixed terrain. Fallen soldiers are replaced by fresh recruits queuing on the hill at the start screen, while the dead get their own gravestones on Boot Hill.

## The challenge

Troopers are fragile, so a single stray bullet, a mine, your own grenade blast or wandering into water or quicksand can wipe out soldiers who have survived for missions. The game tests careful positioning, handling of scarce explosives, and splitting the squad well, and the later missions get very hard. Losing your whole squad sends a fresh, low-ranked batch to redo the phase.

## What makes it unique

Sensible Software paired cute, tiny sprites and a jaunty theme song ('War has never been so much fun') with a bleak anti-war message, shown by the growing graveyard of named recruits. The quick mouse controls made real-time squad tactics easy to pick up, and the game drew tabloid controversy for using the remembrance poppy on its cover.

## Remake angle

Top-down diorama style (tilt-shift miniature look) fits perfectly: little soldiers on lush jungle, snow and desert terrain, destructible huts, water shaders, and the famous graveyard hill between missions.

Destructible jungles and buildings, volumetric explosions, dynamic water and weather, plus a detailed, lit Boot Hill memorial, would all hit harder at high fidelity. Online co-op with each player running their own sub-squad would fit the existing split-team system naturally.

## Map compatibility

Read the original CF1/CF2 map (`.map`) and sprite-placement (`.spt`) files as Open Fodder (GPL-3.0) does, plus OpenFodder Editor output. Open Fodder also runs the free Amiga cover-disk demo data, which is legal test material.

## Existing free versions

The only serious free option (Open Fodder) is an engine reimplementation that needs the original data and keeps pixel visuals; no free or commercial modern-presentation take on the formula exists.
