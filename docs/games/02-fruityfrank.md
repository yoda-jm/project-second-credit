# 2. Fruity Frank (1984)

*Original: Kuma Computers · Amstrad CPC · Maze / Dig. Remake effort: small. Free-version gap: **wide open**.*

The final title must be an original name (see [../legal.md](../legal.md)).

## Core loop

Frank tunnels through his garden collecting fruit, drops apples on the monsters and throws a bouncing ball to stop them.

## Gameplay

Frank moves through a garden made of soil, digging tunnels as he goes, and collects the fruit scattered around the screen. Monsters follow him through the tunnels. He can throw a ball that bounces along the passages and knocks out a monster, and apples can be undermined so they fall and squash whatever is below. Clearing all the fruit or all the monsters ends the level.

## Levels

Single-screen garden levels that get faster and more crowded as you progress, with the tunnel layout reshaped by the player's own digging each time.

## The challenge

You open new paths for the monsters as you dig, so every tunnel is a trade-off. The ball has to come back before you can throw again, which leaves you exposed if you miss.

## What makes it unique

A home-computer take on the Mr. Do! formula that became one of the best-loved Amstrad CPC games, especially in France. Its bright, fruity look and bouncy music gave it a charm that stood out on the CPC.

## Remake angle

A lush vegetable-garden diorama: soft soil you carve through, glossy fruit, cartoon monsters. Shares its dig-and-crush tech with Boulder Dash and Dig Dug, so it's cheap once one of those exists.

Deformable soil with visible cross-sections, squashy physics on falling apples, and a bouncing ball with a glowing trail. Warm garden lighting and a day-to-dusk cycle across levels.
