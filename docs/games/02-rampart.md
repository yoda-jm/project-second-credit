# 2. Rampart (1990)

*Original: Atari Games · Arcade · Puzzle / Strategy. Remake effort: small. Free-version gap: **wide open**.*

The final title must be an original name (see [../legal.md](../legal.md)).

## Core loop

Build walls from Tetris-like pieces to enclose castles, place cannons inside your territory, bombard ships or rivals, repeat.

## Gameplay

Rampart mixes strategy, puzzle and action in rounds of three phases. First the player places cannons inside their castle walls, then fires them with a cursor at attacking ships (single player) or at rival players' walls and cannons (multiplayer). Finally, under a timer, the player repairs the damage by placing Tetris-like wall pieces to enclose at least one castle; castles enclosed earn territory and more cannons.

## Levels

The single-player game is a sequence of battles against increasingly numerous and powerful ships, which also land grunts that roam the player's land and block building. The arcade version supports up to three players in competitive battles.

## The challenge

Pressure comes from the short timer during rebuilding: players fail when they cannot close their walls around a castle in time. It tests fast spatial puzzle skills and aiming, and later waves bring sturdier ships and more damage to repair.

## What makes it unique

Rampart fused real-time castle defense with Tetris-style building years before the tower-defense genre existed. Its multiplayer duels, where each player's cannon fire becomes the other's puzzle, are its signature.

## Remake angle

A three-phase loop in one sentence. Modern destruction physics, splashing water and 4-player online play would give it a lot of room to grow. ChatGPT's favourite.

Detailed medieval coastlines with dynamic water, cannon smoke, fire and physically crumbling stone walls would make battles spectacular. Online multiplayer and a readable, tactile building phase would keep the core rules intact.

## Map compatibility

None to import: the arcade maps are fixed. Our coastlines and castle layouts will be data files (our own format, CC BY-SA), so new maps can be shared as packs.
