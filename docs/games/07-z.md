# 7. Z (1996)

*Original: The Bitmap Brothers · DOS / Mac · RTS. Remake effort: large. Free-version gap: **partly covered**.*

The final title must be an original name (see [../legal.md](../legal.md)).

## Core loop

No base building: capture territory flags and every factory, radar and gun in the sector becomes yours. More territory means faster production. Robot armies fight for the map.

## Gameplay

In this real-time strategy game, the player controls a robot army and captures territories by planting flags in sectors. Captured sectors add factories and increase unit production speed, and the goal is to destroy the enemy fort or capture its flag. Units include robot infantry types and vehicles such as jeeps, tanks and missile launchers, and gun emplacements can be captured and used.

## Levels

The single-player campaign is organised into planets with distinct terrain such as desert, volcanic, arctic, jungle and city worlds, with levels growing larger and tougher. Humorous cutscenes featuring the robots Brad and Allan and their commander appear between levels.

## The challenge

The game is fast and aggressive, with very little room for slow build-up: the AI pushes hard for territory, and keeping production up while defending and attacking takes quick decisions. Losing sectors quickly slows production and can snowball into defeat.

## What makes it unique

Z was unusual in rewarding territory control rather than resource gathering, and in its irreverent humour and characterful units. The Bitmap Brothers' style and the fast pace set it apart from contemporaries like Command & Conquer.

## Remake angle

A fast, aggressive RTS with a ton of personality (the sarcastic robots Brad and Allan, cutscenes). Modern tilt-shift battlefields, destructible terrain, great unit animation. Its compact ruleset suits a solo or AI-driven project better than a full RTS.

Detailed, destructible terrain with dynamic lighting and particle effects for explosions and weapons, plus expressive robot animations, would suit a high-end remake. Online multiplayer and ranked matches would fit the fast, aggressive design.

## Map compatibility

Read Zod Engine maps (GPL-3.0; the fenio fork has 34 campaign maps and a procedural skirmish generator). Original Z data importer: format still to investigate.

## Existing free versions

An open-source playable Z clone exists (Zod Engine + SDL3 fork) but it's pixel-faithful; no free version with modern lighting/effects, and the commercial 2014 HD remake is modest.

- **The Zod Engine** — https://zod.sourceforge.net/ (https://sourceforge.net/projects/zod/) — GPL-3.0 · faithful clone · look: retro-faithful · last activity 2018 · original levels: partial (recreated Z-style maps; original campaign maps in fork) · custom levels: yes (map editor + tile editor). C++/SDL/OpenGL remake of Z with online-persistent multiplayer; uses Z-like sprite art (asset provenance unverified).
- **zod (fenio fork, SDL3)** — https://github.com/fenio/zod — GPL-3.0 · faithful clone · look: retro-faithful · last activity 2025 · original levels: yes (34 campaign maps) · custom levels: yes (zod_mapgen procedural skirmish generator). Modernized build: SDL3, HiDPI, scaled framebuffer, smoothing; still pixel-art presentation.
- **Qt_ZodEngine (BallWin)** — https://github.com/BallWin/Qt_ZodEngine — GPL-3.0 (inherited, unverified) · faithful clone · look: retro-faithful · last activity unknown · original levels: unknown · custom levels: unknown. Qt-based rework of the Zod Engine.

Official or commercial versions: Z The Game (2014, TickTock Games / KISS / Kavcom; Steam, rebuilt HD version, also mobile) and Z: Steel Soldiers (2001 3D sequel; Steam re-release 2014 with enhanced graphics). Rebellion acquired the Bitmap Brothers catalogue in 2019. Nothing with premium modern presentation.
